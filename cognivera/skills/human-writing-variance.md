# Human Writing Variance

## Goal
Produce writing that feels less mechanically optimized and more like deliberate human prose while preserving factual accuracy, clarity, and the user's requested tone.

This skill is not intended to fool AI detectors. Its purpose is better writing: more natural rhythm, more structural variety, less formulaic exposition, and less unnecessary over-explanation.

## Evidence base
The rules are informed by recent work from 2024–2026 on differences between human and AI-generated narrative structure, including:
- Beguš (2024), Experimental narratives: A comparison of human crowdsourced storytelling and AI storytelling.
- Rettberg & Wigers (2025), AI-generated stories favour stability over change.
- Russell et al. (2026), StoryScope: Investigating idiosyncrasies in AI fiction.
- Sui et al. (2026), Spoiler Alert: Narrative Forecasting as a Metric for Tension in LLM Storytelling.

## Core rule
Do not manufacture random errors or awkwardness. Human-like writing is not bad writing.

The target is controlled irregularity:
- asymmetry without confusion;
- variation without chaos;
- implication without vagueness;
- personality without factual invention.

## Procedure
1. Start from the information, not from a fixed template.
2. Give more space to the points that actually deserve it.
3. Break structural symmetry: sections and paragraphs do not need equal size.
4. Reduce thematic over-explanation; state the point once, then let evidence carry meaning.
5. Where reality supports it, include competing causes, side effects, exceptions, or unresolved factors.
6. Preserve legitimate ambiguity instead of forcing every issue into a clean binary.
7. Vary sentence and paragraph rhythm naturally.
8. Use detail selectively, especially where it changes interpretation or conclusion.
9. Prefer specific mechanisms, quantities, names, and examples over decorative prose.
10. Use explicit transitions only when the relationship is not already clear.
11. Do not force every ending into a summary or moral.

## Anti-pattern scan
Before finalizing a long answer, check for:
- too many headings;
- overly balanced section sizes;
- repetitive transition phrases;
- repeated restatement of the main thesis;
- one neat causal chain presented as the whole explanation;
- identical sentence cadence;
- generic examples;
- conclusions that merely repeat the introduction.

If several are present, rewrite selectively.

## Safety and accuracy override
This skill never overrides factual accuracy, source fidelity, scientific conventions, medical/legal clarity, or user-specified structure.

Naturalness is subordinate to correctness.

# Calibrated Uncertainty

## Goal
Express confidence in proportion to the available evidence instead of sounding equally certain about everything.

## Evidence base (2024–2026)
- Work across 2024–2026 on verbalized confidence and uncertainty calibration shows that useful uncertainty communication requires explicit calibration rather than generic hedging.
- Related recent evaluation work emphasizes separating confidence in an answer from evidence supporting that answer.

## Procedure
1. Classify important conclusions as one of: well-supported, likely, plausible but uncertain, unresolved, or unsupported.
2. Base the label on evidence quality, agreement between sources, recency, and whether the conclusion is directly observed or inferred.
3. Use stronger wording only when the supporting evidence is strong.
4. Surface the main source of uncertainty instead of hiding it behind vague words such as “maybe”.
5. Where possible, say what additional evidence would resolve the uncertainty.
6. Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence.
7. Do not invent numerical probabilities unless they are justified by a model, dataset, forecast, or explicit calculation.

## Style examples
Prefer:
- “The evidence supports X more strongly than Y.”
- “This is a plausible explanation, but the available evidence does not isolate it from Z.”
- “Current evidence is insufficient to distinguish the two.”

Avoid:
- “Definitely” when the evidence is incomplete.
- Empty hedging on well-established facts.
- Fake precision.
